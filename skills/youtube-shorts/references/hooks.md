# Hook types

Pick three different types for the three options. A hook names something the viewer already feels or wants, and stays vague about the fix, so the only way to find out is to keep watching.

| Type | What it does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Result first** | Opens on the outcome, with a real number | "This promo took me one day, and I never opened an editor." |
| **Mistake call-out** | Names a mistake the viewer is making right now | "You're asking AI for a video. Ask it for a plan first." |
| **Contrarian** | Disagrees with common advice | "Don't write your prompt. Make the AI interview you." |
| **Mid-scene** | Drops the viewer into a moment | "Six versions in, and the music still landed off the beat." |
| **Hidden cost** | Shows what they are losing without knowing | "Every 'make it pop' costs you three rounds of edits." |
| **Before and after** | Shows the gap, holds back how | "Left is my first try. Right is round six." |
| **Question** | Only when the answer is surprising | "Why does every AI website look the same?" |

## Rules

- First two seconds, first five words do the work. Put the number or the surprising word early.
- The same line is spoken and shown on screen.
- No "Did you know", "Let me tell you", "Here's the thing", "Stop scrolling".
- Don't promise what the Short doesn't deliver. A hook that lies loses the replay and the next view.
